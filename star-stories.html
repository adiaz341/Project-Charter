Project Presentation Stories

Adding a Job Description Deliverable

Source: Project Presentation

Situation

During our semester-long Project Management project, I focused heavily on deliverables that would help me learn core concepts. Partway through the semester, I realized that none of my deliverables actually checked whether what I was learning would matter in the job market. This created a risk that I could invest time in skills that might not help me get an entry-level IT role.

Task

After discussing this with my professor, she suggested adding a deliverable that compared my learning plan to real job requirements. My task was to research entry-level IT roles and determine whether my learning path aligned with industry needs.

Action

I researched postings for cybersecurity, networking, and help desk roles, collected required skills, and compared them to my coursework. I documented similarities, gaps, and patterns, then evaluated how Cisco Networking Academy aligned with employer expectations.

Result

The analysis confirmed that my chosen paths supported job readiness. I also identified resources that help prepare for CompTIA A+, Network+, and Security+. The deliverable ensured my learning was industry-aligned rather than theoretical.

Making Daisy the STAR of My STORY

Source: Project Presentation

Situation

For our semester project, we were assigned to create a short video demonstrating something we learned. I wanted to explain STAR stories but felt uncomfortable being on camera, which left me uncertain about how to complete the assignment effectively.

Task

I needed to create a clear, creative video explaining the STAR method while avoiding direct on-camera presentation. My wife suggested using our puppy, Daisy, as the “star” of the video and recording a voiceover.

Action

I wrote a script explaining the STAR method and filmed Daisy “typing” each letter using treats for motivation. I combined the footage with a voiceover to explain the concept in simple, engaging terms.

Result

The video was fun, charming, and well received by viewers. It allowed me to complete the assignment comfortably while still explaining the STAR method effectively.

PMI Principles Stories

Building Trust with a New Client

Source: PMI Principle 1 – Be a Diligent, Respectful, and Caring Steward

Prompt: Stakeholder Trust Building

Situation

We had a new client selling detergent in small packages who wanted to evaluate our ability to produce 10,000 units within a month. To determine feasibility, we ran a sample batch to ensure mutual benefit.

Task

As lead powder blender, I needed to ensure we could produce, package, and ship the product safely and efficiently. This required coordinating staffing and addressing dust ventilation concerns.

Action

I prepared materials, ensured proper respirator use, mixed the product, and coordinated workflow across production and packaging. Communication remained constant to maintain safety and quality.

Result

The client was highly satisfied with both quality and speed. He continued working with us and even provided a bonus. The experience strengthened trust and demonstrated our reliability under pressure.

Substituting Suitable Ingredients to Complete an Emergency Order

Source: PMI Principle 9 – Navigating Complexity

Prompt: Adaptive Planning

Situation

A plating company urgently needed a soaking powder by the end of the day, but we were missing a key ingredient required for its spreading function. Without it, the order couldn’t be fulfilled.

Task

I needed to identify a safe and effective substitute immediately. After consulting my dad, who has 30+ years of blending experience, I compared SDS sheets and confirmed we already had an equivalent material.

Action

I presented my findings to the general manager, obtained approval, produced the batch, and submitted a QC sample to verify consistency before packaging.

Result

We delivered the product within an hour. The customer thanked us for the quick turnaround and confirmed it worked perfectly, reinforcing trust and showcasing adaptability.

Assuming Leadership Unofficially

Source: PMI Principle 6 – Leading Without Authority

Situation

During a group project, communication stopped entirely for two weeks, and no one was completing their assigned tasks. With deadlines approaching, the project was at risk of failure.

Task

I took initiative to reestablish communication, create a Teams channel, contact members directly, and notify the professor so expectations were clear and progress could resume.

Action

I sent an urgent message outlining deadlines and the need to regroup. I stated that if no one responded, I would complete the project alone while documenting my work. This prompted the team to reengage, allowing us to reassign roles and set new internal deadlines.

Result

We completed the project and earned a B+. Although lower than hoped, it taught me that delayed communication harms outcomes, and proactive leadership is essential.
