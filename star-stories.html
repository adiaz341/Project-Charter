STAR Stories

Your collection of behavioral interview stories using the STAR method.

Submission Format

Create a STAR story for each prompt using the required elements below.

  • Story Title: Clear, descriptive title
  • Source: Indicate if it’s from Project Presentation or specify the PMI Principle number and name
  • Prompt (for PMI stories): Include the specific prompt you’re responding to
  • STAR Story: Clearly labeled Situation, Task, Action, and Result

💡 Organization Tips

  • Group stories by source (Project Presentations vs. PMI Principles).
  • Use the same format for every story on this page.
  • Keep the STAR headings visible for each story.
  • Use the dividers between stories to make scanning easier.
  • If you include an optional risk/issue story, label it clearly as Optional.

⭐ STAR Story
Adding a Job Description Deliverable

Source: Project Presentation

Prompt (if PMI story): —

Situation

During our semester-long Project Management project, I focused heavily on deliverables that would help me learn core concepts. Partway through the semester, I realized that none of my deliverables actually checked whether what I was learning would matter in the job market. This created a risk that I could invest a lot of time in skills that might not help me get an entry-level IT role.

Task

After discussing this with my professor, she suggested I add a deliverable that compared my learning plan to real job requirements. My task was to research entry-level IT positions and determine whether the paths I was pursuing would make me a stronger candidate, or whether I needed to adjust my focus.

Action

That evening, I researched job postings for entry-level roles such as cybersecurity analyst, network analyst, and help desk support. I reviewed openings from multiple companies and captured the required skills, certifications, and tools in a Word document. I then looked for patterns across postings, grouped similar requirements, and compared them against the courses I was taking through Cisco’s Networking Academy and my other study plans.

Result

The comparison confirmed that the networking basics, IT support, and cybersecurity analyst paths I had chosen were closely aligned with what employers were asking for. Using ChatGPT, I also identified additional resources and mapped out a path toward CompTIA A+, Network+, and Security+ certifications. This new deliverable gave me confidence that my coursework and self-study are building directly toward real entry-level IT opportunities rather than just classroom knowledge.

⭐ STAR Story
Making Daisy the STAR of My STORY

Source: Project Presentation

Prompt (if PMI story): —

Situation

As part of our semester project, we were assigned to create a short “TikTok-style” video demonstrating something we learned in class. I initially wanted to explain STAR stories, but I faced a major obstacle: I’m not comfortable being on camera and felt awkward filming myself. I didn’t know how to make the assignment both engaging and personally manageable.

Task

My task was to produce a creative and clear video demonstrating the STAR method. I needed to find a way to meet the project’s expectations without relying on traditional on-camera presentation. While brainstorming ideas with my wife, she suggested a unique and unexpected solution—feature our puppy, Daisy, as the “star” of the video and use a voiceover for the explanation.

Action

I wrote a simple script that explained the STAR acronym in an accessible way. My wife helped develop the concept of having Daisy “type out” each letter of STAR in the video. Using treats for motivation, Daisy performed her role perfectly while I recorded the visuals and added a voiceover narration to describe the method.

Result

The final video was fun, creative, and well received. Even though I worried it wasn’t highly technical, viewers—including family members we tested it with—loved it, largely thanks to Daisy’s charm. The process allowed me to complete the assignment in a way that felt authentic and comfortable, while still effectively communicating what STAR stories are.

⭐ STAR Story
Building Trust with a New Client

Source: PMI Principle 1 – Be a Diligent, Respectful, and Caring Steward

Prompt (for PMI stories): Stakeholder Trust Building

Situation

In my current job, we welcomed a new client who sells laundry detergent in small packaging quantities through Amazon. He was considering a large order of 10,000 units, but before committing, both sides needed to confirm whether we could reliably produce and package the material within a one-month window. To assess feasibility, we agreed to run a sample batch that would inform whether the partnership would be beneficial for both parties.

Task

As the lead powder blender, I was responsible for determining whether we could meet the production and packaging requirements. I first aligned with my supervisor on the timeline and staffing needs, identifying which employees had the skills and reliability to assist with the packaging phase. We also evaluated the material’s characteristics and recognized that the dry mix created a dust issue that would need proper ventilation. Together, we established a production approach that emphasized worker safety, efficiency, and quality.

Action

With the plan in place, I coordinated the preparation of both materials and equipment. I ensured employees used N95 respirators to reduce inhalation risks from the dust. I tested the mixture process and found that the formulation wasn’t difficult and could be blended in roughly 30 minutes. After mixing, we transported the product to the packaging area, maintaining consistent communication to keep the workflow safe, clean, and efficient.

Result

The client was fully satisfied with the sample batch and impressed with how quickly and professionally we produced it. He expressed appreciation by providing a small bonus to our company and moved forward with us as a trusted supplier. This experience not only strengthened a valuable customer relationship but also demonstrated our team’s capability to deliver high-quality results under new and uncertain conditions.

⭐ STAR Story
Substituting Suitable Ingredients to Complete an Emergency Order

Source: PMI Principle 9 – Navigating Complexity

Prompt (for PMI stories): Adaptive Planning

Situation

We received an emergency order from a plating company that urgently needed a soaking powder by the end of the day. The mix was normally simple to produce, but as I began preparing the batch, I realized we were missing a key ingredient responsible for helping the powder “spread” across the metals during washing. Without it, the product would not perform correctly, and the customer’s operations could be delayed.

Task

I needed to find a safe, effective substitute immediately, as there was no time to order the missing ingredient. Drawing on my dad’s 30 years of chemical blending experience as a resource, I consulted with him about potential alternatives. I also examined our inventory and noticed another ingredient with a similar appearance and purpose. After comparing Safety Data Sheets (SDS), I discovered that the substitute material was chemically equivalent to the original ingredient previously used.

Action

Once I confirmed the match, I brought my findings to our general manager to request approval. I showed him the SDS documentation that demonstrated the equivalency, and he agreed that it was an appropriate substitution. Following his direction, I produced the batch and pulled a QC sample for a consistency check before packaging.

Result

We successfully completed and shipped the order within an hour of receiving it. Our general manager notified the customer of the substitution, and later that day the customer replied with appreciation for our quick response. He confirmed that the product worked exactly as expected and had no performance issues. This experience strengthened customer trust and demonstrated our capability to adapt quickly and safely under urgent, complex conditions.

⭐ STAR Story
Assuming Leadership Unofficially

Source: PMI Principle 6 – Leading Without Authority

Prompt (for PMI stories): —

Situation

Last semester, I was assigned to a group of five classmates for a major project. Since we didn’t know each other beforehand, we spent the first few weeks establishing roles and dividing responsibilities. However, by the fourth week, communication within the group had abruptly stopped. For two weeks, no one responded inside or outside of class, and I had no visibility into whether anyone was completing their tasks. With deadlines approaching, it became clear that if no one stepped up, the project would fall apart.

Task

To prevent us from missing deadlines and receiving a poor grade, I took the initiative to reestablish communication. I created a Microsoft Teams channel for the group and sent a message through Canvas to ensure everyone received it. I wasn’t willing to let the group’s lack of communication put my grade at risk, so I drafted a clear email outlining the need to regroup, along with a proposed plan. I also informed the professor of the situation so he understood that I had been working independently up to that point.

Action

In my message, I emphasized the urgency of the deadlines and made it clear that we needed to organize ourselves immediately. I also explained that if no one responded, I would complete the project on my own and submit documentation to the professor to reflect my effort. This prompted the group to reply, and once communication resumed, I facilitated the reassignment of roles, set internal deadlines, and delegated tasks based on each member’s strengths. I continued monitoring progress to ensure we stayed on track.

Result

Thanks to the renewed structure and clarity, we completed the entire project. Although the final grade—a B+—was lower than what I had hoped for, I was proud that we pulled the project together under pressure. The experience taught me that waiting too long to intervene can significantly damage group outcomes—as seen in our rushed PowerPoint and limited preparation time. Now, when I face similar issues in group work, I address communication problems immediately and take initiative rather than hoping they resolve on their own.
